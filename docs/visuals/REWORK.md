# Visual rework: a realistic stadium, code only

Goal: the look of the owner's concept images (a floodlit night stadium in a harbour, wet striped
grass, a steel cage with wire netting, floodlight towers, stands with a crowd, banners and LED
boards, a city and harbour behind; and the same stadium in other settings), with **no asset
files** (every mesh, texture and sound is generated by this repository's code) and **without making
the game slow**.

It is based on a measured prototype (a throwaway lab outside the repo, 2026-10-20). That study scored
closeness to the concept at 12/100 for the game before this work and 48/100 for the first prototype.
It estimates about 70/100 is reachable on the high tier (about 3 ms of GPU at 1600x900 on a Turing
desktop GPU; the game used 1.2 ms) and about 60/100 on the low tier (about 1.2 ms).

## Quality tiers (`src/render/quality.ts`)

Four tiers, one table of switches (`TIERS`) that every view reads:

| | super low (opt-in, auto on weak TVs) | low (Performance) | high (default on desktop) | ultra (opt-in) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| path | low's direct path | straight to the canvas (no HDR target, no MRT, no post pass) | HDR pipeline | HDR pipeline |
| backend | WebGL2 (`#webgpu`: WebGPU), chosen before the renderer exists | any | any | any |
| AA | none | the canvas' MSAA x4 (none in WebGPU compatibility mode: a blit resolve there) | MSAA x4 (FXAA without a multisampled HDR target) | MSAA x4 (idem) |
| bloom | none | none (glow from geometry) | emissive bloom | emissive bloom |
| resolution | about 640x360 (a 230k pixel budget below 1:1), fixed; 30 fps at most | pixel ratio up to 1.5 in a 1 Mpx budget + dynamic resolution (0.6..1 of it) | pixel budget + dynamic resolution | bigger budget + dynamic resolution |
| shadows | none (no shadow code at all) | none (ground blobs) | 2 keys, 2048 | 2 keys, 4096 |
| lighting | low's, with Lambert / unlit cars, ball and goals (no environment map) | its own: turf, stands, steel, boards unlit with the theme's light baked in; brighter keys and fill on the lit cars, ball and goals | lit, the theme's keys and fill | idem |
| turf | low's unlit turf from a 64² grain (no blade field, no field map), fixed stripes | unlit: the theme's lit green, stripes, markings, pools at a low contrast (no wear, damp, sheen, streaks) | relief, puddles, 4 streak heads | + grass blades near the camera |
| post | low's look in every material | exposure, grade, tone curve (in every material; no contrast or vignette stage) | grade, vignette | + GTAO, SSR on wet turf, lens glare |
| stadium | no netting, no glare, no crowd mesh (people painted on the rows) | no netting (the steel draws the cage), a bright flat crowd | full | + floodlight beams |
| world | a gradient dome from the theme's sky (no bake, no fog, no live layer) | the alpine valley's lighter live layer (~52k triangles) | full | full |

Phones and tablets start on low, desktops on high; the WebGL2 fallback, WebGPU in compatibility
mode and devices with `navigator.deviceMemory` <= 4 start on low unless the player chose a tier, a
device with 1 GB or a TV (no pointer) on a slow GPU starts on super low (`bootTier.ts`, "Super low"
below), and no device starts above the tier its GPU last fell back to (`tierGuard.ts`). Every tier runs as
`adjustTier(TIERS[q], capabilities)` (`capabilities.ts`): the menu offers every tier, a device that
cannot do one fully runs it without the missing pieces (see "Three devices" below). Saves from
before stored 0 (high) or 1 (performance). Low has no shadow maps
(since 2026-10-21): the cars and the ball keep their ground blobs, and no material samples a shadow
map (see "Owner feedback" below).

## Themes (`src/render/theme.ts`)

A theme is data: the same generators and shaders read it. Shared facts live in `theme.ts`: the two key
lights, fill, floodlights, wetness, fog, tone mapping and grade. Each view keeps its own per-theme
parameters next to its code, keyed by `ThemeId`. The light count and material variants never change
between themes, so a theme switch only re-bakes, rewrites uniforms and repaints the board
canvas; it never recompiles shaders. Gameplay never reads a theme.

Themes: `harbour-night` (the default), `canyon-sunset`, `alpine-day`. The Stadium
row picks one (saved with the prefs; an unknown saved id reads as the night harbour). Each has a
world preset and a skyline (harbour, canyon or alpine: a baked far part and, except the canyon, a
live near layer that the world swaps in by geometry), a ThemeWorld, a stadium palette, a turf look
and its place's board content (`stadium/content.ts`: invented local sponsors, names and
canvas-path emblems, and the place's main and secondary sponsor for the big side-wall boards; the harbour's names and emblems appear nowhere else).

`city-dusk`, `railyard-night` and `harbour-day` were removed on 2026-10-21: the owner keeps three
stadiums "for now" (the city and the rail yard felt too bright and reflective). `harbour-day` was
only data on the harbour generator: its ThemeWorld, world preset, stadium palette and turf look are
in commit 32aabad. To bring the city and the rail yard back, start from the same commit: `world/city.ts`,
`world/railyard.ts`, `world/railyardMaterial.ts`, their KIND ranges (TOWER / DECK, BRICK /
RAILBED) and branches in `bakeMaterials.ts`, the world's per-skyline ground / proxy overrides,
their ThemeWorld, preset, palette, turf look, content set, emblems (skyline, neonStar, wheel, gear,
chimney), menu names and `tests/railyard.test.mjs`.

## Module layout

| folder | owns | built from |
|---|---|---|
| `src/render/world/` | sky (gradient, clouds, stars, moon or sun), backdrop generators (harbour, canyon...), the bake of both into a cube map + PMREM environment, cached in IndexedDB; super low's lite world (liteWorld.ts: a gradient dome, nothing baked) | the old sky dome + environment |
| `src/render/turf/` | pitch material (blade texture baked at load, stripes, wear, wetness, puddles, floodlight pools and streaks, relief), grass blades (ultra) | the old floor material |
| `src/render/stadium/` | cage (steel arches, hex wire netting on the shell), LED boards with a big sponsor board on each side wall (each place's own content; the banners went on 2026-10-21), stands and crowd, floodlight towers and lamp heads, beams (ultra) | the old truss, cones, stands, masts, halos, lamp bars, glass |
| `src/render/post/` | render pipeline per tier (bloom, grade, vignette, FXAA, GTAO / SSR / flare on ultra), key lights and shadows | the old `applyQuality` chain |
| `Car.ts`, `Ball.ts` | paint with clearcoat flakes, tyres, glass, the panelled ball | same files |

Goals, goal frames and boost pads stay in `Arena.ts`. The collision mesh (`src/sim/arenaMesh.ts`) does not
change: everything drawn on the walls coincides with the shell or sits outside it, and the roof stays
flat because the physics ceiling is flat.

## Contracts every module keeps

- WebGPU (also in compatibility mode) and the automatic WebGL2 fallback (TSL only; compute needs a
  fallback path).
- Glow: bloom reads only the `emissive` MRT target. Every unlit or transparent material sets
  `mrtNode = glowMrt(...)` or `noGlowMrt()` (`materials.ts`). On low there is no bloom and no glow
  target (both return null there), so glow must come from geometry and colour. Low blends in
  display space (after the look): an additive material's black stays black (no lift: pipeline.ts
  `finish` leaves it out of additive materials) and its quad fades to zero in a round shape.
- NaN hygiene: saturate every uv, fade and `pow` base. Verify with `#nancheck`.
- The scene renders through the tier's path only (`post/pipeline.ts`): the HDR pipeline's PassNode
  (high, ultra; tone mapping and sRGB once in `renderOutput`) or the direct path (low; the same
  look and sRGB at the end of every material, three's DirectRenderPipeline). Never a plain
  `renderer.render(scene, camera)`.
- No allocation per frame. Static work is baked at load behind the loading overlay. Hidden meshes
  get a warm-up hook (`Game.setWarm`).
- Draw calls: at most 16 on super low, 40 on low and 60 on high. One draw per module element is the
  house style (instance or merge). Super low boots on 18 programs whatever the roster (cars share
  their body, wheel and flame programs there: `SHARED_INSTANCING_CAPACITY`, uniform team tints); on
  WebGL2 a roster change (a mode, training) still links 5 again, as every tier does (see "Review of
  super low" below).
- Phones: everything the low tier and the WebGL2 fallback use must work on a low-end mobile GPU.
  `#lowgpu` in the URL emulates one on a desktop (WebGL2 without OES_texture_float_linear,
  EXT_color_buffer_float, EXT_float_blend..., capped limits), `#compat` a WebGPU device in
  compatibility mode (Chrome on an OpenGL ES phone: no multisampled rgba16float, 4096 textures,
  15 varyings, 16 KB uniform buffers, 4 colour targets), `#nof32filter` one without
  'float32-filterable' (Safari on an iPhone), `#nomsaa` every tier without multisampling (an A/B
  of the canvas MSAA's cost on a phone); `#diag` shows the backend, GPU, extensions, limits,
  capabilities, the tier as the device runs it, the remembered ceiling and every shader or GPU
  error on screen (the first ones stay; a failed command buffer names its passes), for a
  screenshot from the phone.
- A tier that breaks on a device falls back one tier by itself (`tierGuard.ts`), never in a loop;
  a device lost in the background (or on super low) reloads on the same tier, through the tab's
  counter (reload, then super low, then a message: "Super low" below).
- Gameplay readability: markings, goal frames, boost pads, ball marker and car silhouettes stay
  legible. Team colours keep their colour-blind separation.
- Original content only. No commercial game names, logos, car designs or layouts. Board
  text is invented and drawn with code.

## Owner feedback (2026-10-21), the render track

The owner tried a preview on phones. Changes (render side; the loading screens, the settings in
the pause menu and the ball's shadow by day are the UI track's):

- **A phone that drew no pitch** (Huawei, Kirin 710F / Mali-G51, Android; an iPhone 15 was fine):
  on low the pitch was dark grey without markings, the car a blob without a body, no ball. Those
  are the only three materials that receive shadows, so the one path they share is the shadow-map
  sampling; three r186 even takes an Android-only depth-compare fallback for it on WebGPU, and the
  WebGL2 path samples a `sampler2DShadow` in dynamic branches. The float formats were audited and
  ruled out: every texture and target the low tier and WebGL2 use is RGBA8 or half float (blade
  field, field and pool maps, light map, world cube / PMREM / proxy, board atlas, post targets),

  ball keep their ground blobs), which also frees the 34 MB shadow map and the shadow pass on
  phones. Not reproducible on a desktop driver: `#diag` on the phone will tell (backend, GPU,
  extensions, limits, any shader or GPU error).
- **Boards**: a big board three panels wide (~31 m, the same height) in the middle of each side
  wall alternates between the place's main and secondary sponsor every 30 s, half a period out of
  step with the other boards; every board changes every 30 s instead of 8, a whole panel at a
  time, with a short dip instead of a slide. The panels tile the outline exactly (36). Same
  material, texture and draw. The LED face is satin (its gloss mirrored the lamp heads across the
  names).
- **Calmer night pitch**: the night harbour is damp, not soaked (wetness 0.32, was 0.55), with
  shallower puddles and fainter streaks, and every tier evaluates half the streak heads (low 2,
  high and ultra 4). On the desktop GPU the frame time is the same within noise (A/B interleaved,
  1600x900, median of 3 x 120 frames: low 0.807 / 0.814 ms, high 1.839 / 1.833, ultra 3.094 /
  3.078); the saving is for ALU-bound phones.
- **Themes**: `city-dusk`, `railyard-night` and `harbour-day` removed (see "Themes"): three
  stadiums remain.
- **No hanging banners** on any stadium (one draw less).

Measured after the changes (same method as the final review, night harbour): low 0.82 ms / 24
draws / 37 MB of textures, high 1.83 ms / 41 / 160 MB, ultra 3.04 ms / 52 / 374 MB.

## Three devices (2026-10-21, second round): capabilities, a safety net, a direct Performance tier

The owner tested the preview on an Intel Mac (Safari: WebGL2; Chrome: WebGPU), an iPhone (Safari,
WebGPU without 'float32-filterable') and a Huawei with a Kirin 710F (Mali, Android 10, Chrome in
WebGPU compatibility mode, 4 GB). The Mac was fine. The iPhone ran Performance (dark) and broke on
Ultra ("GPUCommandEncoder.finish: encoder state is 'Locked'"); the Huawei ran Performance and broke
on High ("Invalid Texture 'output-msaa'"). The owner: Performance must run fluidly on every device
and look good, not dark or blotchy; take things out of it if needed rather than imitate High.

- **The Huawei's High, reproduced** with `#compat` (the adapter three requests hides
  'core-features-and-limits', so Dawn validates like the phone, with the same default limits):
  rgba16float "does not support multisampling" in compatibility mode, so the MSAA x4 HDR scene
  pass ('output-msaa', 'emissive-msaa') was invalid every frame. Fix: capability gating
  (`capabilities.ts`): without `msaaHdr` the post tiers take FXAA. High and ultra then run clean in
  compatibility mode (three warns that per-target MRT blending falls back to the material's there).
- **The iPhone's Ultra: not reproducible on Chromium.** `#nof32filter` runs every tier clean: no tier
  binds a float32 colour texture (every target and baked texture is RGBA8 or half float, depth
  textures are bound as depth). 'Locked' is WebKit's state of an encoder whose render pass was
  invalid or never ended; the first error was not on the panel. Candidates: one of the ultra passes
  WebKit validates differently (the half-depth copy, the SSR blur into mip levels) or memory (ultra
  holds 374 MB of textures, 268 MB of it the two 4096 shadow maps). Now: the safety net falls back to
  High on that phone, and `#diag` keeps the first errors and names the passes of a failed command
  buffer (texture label, format, samples, mip; '(open)' for a pass never ended), so the next
  screenshot names the culprit.
- **Safety net** (`tierGuard.ts`, `Game.onGpuFailure`): GPU validation errors, failed pipelines or
  WebGL programs within 20 s of the boot or a tier switch, or a lost device at any time, make the
  tier fall back one step (ultra -> high -> low) behind the loading screen, with "Your device does
  not support Ultra: using High" / "Tu dispositivo no soporta Ultra: se usa Alta" on the loading
  line and as a menu toast. The highest tier that has not failed is remembered per GPU in
  localStorage (`turbogol.tierCeiling.v1`, 30 days) and caps the starting tier. A lost device
  reloads the page onto the lower tier. Only downward, a failure during a fallback is the
  fallback's own, nothing below low: no loops. Tested by forcing the multisampled HDR target in
  compatibility mode (high -> low, the cause first on `#diag`), real validation errors (ultra ->
  high -> low -> stays) and a simulated device loss (reload onto low, the toast after it).
- **Performance renders straight to the canvas** (`TierSpec.output: 'direct'`, three's
  DirectRenderPipeline): no HalfFloat scene target, no MRT, no composite, no FXAA; the theme's
  vignette, exposure, grade, tone curve and sRGB run at the end of every material's fragment shader
  (output targets only: bakes and shadow maps are untouched), and the canvas multisamples (x4: the
  8-bit canvas does so in compatibility mode too; on the WebGL2 fallback the context is created
  multisampled). `glowMrt` / `noGlowMrt` return null on tiers without bloom; the world's dome, beam
  and live layer, kept across tier switches, are re-tagged (`keepGlowTag`). One 8-bit pass instead
  of a half-float scene target plus two full-screen passes: the bandwidth a phone's tiled GPU saves;
  on the desktop GPU it is the same time within noise, with MSAA x4 instead of FXAA. Blending
  happens after the tone curve there (display space): additive glows add up brighter. The low tier
  renders up to 1.5x on phones (1 Mpx budget: desktops and tablets stay at 1x) and dynamic
  resolution scales that pixel ratio down to 0.7 of it (a phone at 780x360 CSS: 1170x540 down to
  819x378) when frames come late.
- **Default tier**: WebGL2, WebGPU compatibility mode and `deviceMemory` <= 4 start on low unless
  the player chose a tier (phones and tablets already did).
- **Clean switches**: low <-> high <-> ultra round trips keep the texture count, texture memory and
  pipelines flat (low 11 textures / 9.3 MB / 43 pipelines without the canvas buffers, high 29 /
  160 MB / 58, ultra 36 / 374 MB / 66), the canvas' MSAA colour buffer is freed when leaving low on
  WebGPU, theme switches on low compile nothing after each theme's first bake, and the warm-up
  covers every hidden mesh on every tier (pipelines constant over a warm render). On the WebGL2
  fallback the direct tier's parallel compile covers the same programs as the old low path did.

Measured (WebGPU, the reference Turing GPU shared with other sessions, free play at kickoff, chase
camera, night harbour; the old low path A/B'd in the same session by swapping the tier table):

| | drawing buffer | GPU (median) | draws |
|---|---|---|---|
| low, before (FXAA path) | 1600x901 | 0.93 ms | 24 |
| low, now (direct, MSAA x4) | 1600x901 | 0.96 ms (0.85 without MSAA) | 22 |
| low before, phone layout 780x360 CSS at dpr 3 | 780x360 | 0.44 ms | 24 |
| low now, same layout | 1170x540 (1.5x) | 0.55 ms | 22 |
| low now, at the dynamic-resolution floor | 819x378 | 0.33 ms | 22 |
| high | 2190x1233 | 1.81 ms | 41 |
| ultra | 2190x1233 | 3.03 ms | 52 |

(The low rows vary by up to 0.3 ms run to run on the shared GPU; high and ultra did not change.)

What the look of Performance can rely on (for its restyle): the path above; no bloom, no shadow
maps, no screen-space passes, no puddles or relief; glow only from geometry and colour; per theme

vignette) on that tier only (`lookOf`, uniform writes, no recompile); the tone curve is the
theme's (ACES on all three); materials, lights and the turf read the `tier('low')` switches as
before.

## The Performance look (2026-10-21, third round)

The owner, on the same devices: Performance looked "rather dark"; it must run very fluidly on every
device with good visuals, not dark or blotchy, and it need not imitate High (take things out).
What made it dark and blotchy, and what cost a phone's GPU, measured on the old low frame at a
fill-bound size (3200x1802, the per-pixel share a phone pays; each mesh hidden in turn): the lit
turf ~30 % of the frame (four texture reads, the physical model with two keys, the fill and two
PMREM environment reads, the streak loop, the damp sheen), the stand structure ~8 %, the netting
(a transparent layer over most of the upper screen) ~10 %, the crowd ~8 %; the turf's wear, bare
soil, damp patches and sheen read as dark smudges on a dimmed phone screen, the crowd as a dark
mass, and ACES with a 1.18 contrast and a 0.3 vignette crushed the night's darks.

Performance now has its own look (`TierSpec.bakedLight`, `netting`; high and ultra unchanged: high
pixel-diffed against the previous build, only the animated boards, pads, crowd and beam differ;
ultra takes the same code paths):

- **Turf**: unlit (`buildBakedTurfMaterial`): the theme's lit grass colour (`TurfLook.baked`), a
  narrow grain from the blade field (no dark gaps), clean view-dependent mowing stripes, the team
  tint of the halves, crisp self-lit markings, the floodlight pools at a low contrast around a
  mean of 1 (`poolMean`), the goal floors and the goal-mouth glow. No wear, soil, damp, sheen,
  puddles or streaks (`turfStreaks` 0). About a tenth of the lit floor's cost.
- **Stadium**: no netting (the steel frame draws the cage); the stands, steel and boards unlit,
  their colours x the theme's light baked in (`stadiumLight`: both keys' n . l and the hemisphere
  fill, one dot product each, no environment read); a bright, flat crowd palette (clear team kits,
  lighter casual colours, no cheap-volume shading) under each theme's low crowd light
  (`StadiumTheme.low`). Lamp heads and their sprite halos as before (no bloom on this tier).
- **World**: the alpine valley's live layer at a lighter level of detail (`LiveDetail` 'low': the

Fixed:

- **Square patches of light on the night sky (low).** The direct path runs the look at the end of
  every material and blends after it, and the look's lift (night harbour 0.004 / 0.006 / 0.012)
  was added by every ADDITIVE material too, once per layer: each floodlight halo quad, the ball's

  square (`max(|x|, |y|)`): it fades in a circle now (stadium/floodlights.ts, low only).
- **No canvas MSAA in WebGPU compatibility mode (the Huawei).** Dawn's OpenGL ES backend resolves
  a multisampled pass with a framebuffer blit after it, and three stores the canvas' multisampled
  colour and depth on every backend (`storeOp: 'store'`), so a tiled phone GPU writes all four
  samples to memory and reads them back: at 1170x540 about 33 MB per frame instead of about 5,
  roughly 2 GB/s at 60 fps on the weakest phone the game runs on. Even on the desktop GPU the
  canvas MSAA costs a third of the low frame in the phone layout (0.26 ms with it, 0.20 without).
  `capabilities.ts` reads `msaaCanvas` false in compatibility mode, so the direct tier draws
  without anti-aliasing there (its markings are anti-aliased in the shader; `#diag`: `adj msaa
  4 -> 0`). `#msaa` turns it back on there and `#nomsaa` turns every tier's off anywhere, for an
  A/B on the phone itself.
- **Dynamic resolution on low goes down to 0.6** (was 0.7): a phone at 780x360 CSS can drop from
  1170x540 to 702x324 when frames come late (0.26 ms -> 0.13 ms in the phone layout on the desktop
  GPU). It only goes as far down as the late frames need.
- **The remembered ceiling never lifted.** After a fallback the boot started on the lower tier for
  30 days even when the player picked the higher one again and it ran (a fixed build, a driver
  update). A tier the player picks that runs through the 20 s window without a failure now lifts
  a lower ceiling to it (`raiseCeiling`; a failure in the window still falls back and keeps it).
- **A lost device in the background, or on low.** A phone that reclaims the GPU while another app
  is in front loses the device; that dropped the tier for 30 days (on high or ultra) or left a
  frozen picture with nothing to fall back to (on low). A device lost while the page is hidden or
  within 10 s of it coming back, or lost on low, now reloads the page on the same tier without
  touching the ceiling, once a minute at most (the time in sessionStorage: no loop). A loss in the
  foreground on high or ultra still falls back one tier.

The report was checked against the current branch (two independent passes: the code, and three.js

was wrong:

- **Already fixed since that build**: Auto starts on low with `deviceMemory` <= 4, on WebGL2 and in
  WebGPU compatibility mode; low renders straight to the canvas (no HalfFloat scene target, no
  grade RTT, no FXAA: the report's "direct render" was done); the unlit baked turf and stadium, no
  netting on low; device loss hooked into the safety net. None of the report's figures for low
  describe today's low, which on that TV would have run on WebGPU at 1280x720 (maxPixelRatio 1.5
  in a 1 Mpx budget gives the TV's whole dpr) with the canvas MSAA x4 (about 29 MB of
  multisampled colour and depth written out every frame).
- **Held**: the `#lowgpu` black scene (its allowlist kept EXT_color_buffer_half_float, which the TV
  lacks, and dropped EXT_color_buffer_float, which it has: no renderable float format, every
  HalfFloat target incomplete in silence; three never checks framebuffer status); the world still
  bakes and decodes into HalfFloat targets on every load, low included; dynamic resolution blind
  under 10 fps; `#net` matched by any hash containing 'net'; three's WebGL backend always asking for
  an alpha canvas; the TSL shaders highp; a fallback or a GPU's ceiling saved over the player's
  choice by the next prefs write; lost-device reloads gated by a 60 s gap the TV's boot outlasts.

  succeed); the report's flag-parser and replaceState sketches were buggy; "something

  the warm-up frame then compiled the real ones synchronously. That is where the TV's 69 linked

  state anyway). Every WebGL2 boot and tier switch of every tier compiles less now.

### The tier

`superlow` sits below low (QUALITIES: superlow, low, high, ultra; "Super low" / "Súper bajo" in
Settings > Graphics with its hint). It is low's direct path with nothing built that costs:

- **Resolution**: a 230k pixel budget below 1:1 (`SUPERLOW_PIXELS`, quality.ts `pixelRatio`,
  snapped to whole pixels): exactly 640x360 on the TV's 962x541 CSS viewport, a 2x upscale to its
  1280x720 UI surface, and about 640x360 on any other screen. No dynamic resolution, no MSAA (the
  WebGL2 context is created without it).
- **30 fps at most** (`maxFps`, Game.ts `drawsFrame`): the animation frames in between do nothing,
  the next drawn one carries their time (the sim keeps its 120 Hz clock: 4 ticks a frame).
- **Backend**: WebGL2 unless `#webgpu` asks for the A/B (one 8-bit pass, the canvas depth
  discarded before present, no Dawn heaps in the browser's process on a 1 GB device). The backend
  and the world are chosen when the renderer is created, so a switch into or out of super low saves
  the prefs and reloads behind the loading screen (`needsReload`, `Game.restartOn`); low / high /
  ultra still switch in place. In an online match the reload would leave it (an abandon): such a
  pick is saved and waits for the menus (`Game.pendingRestart`; the Graphics tab says "Applies when
  you leave the online match").
- **World** (`world/liteWorld.ts`): a gradient dome from the theme's sky preset (zenith, mid,
  horizon, the glow toward its azimuth, the moon or sun with a short halo) over a flat silhouette
  of the place on the horizon (blocks for the harbour, mesas for the canyon, peaks for the valley,
  from the dome's uv: no texture, no atan). No sky cube, PMREM, live layer, IndexedDB or fog:
  scene.environment stays null and nothing renders into a float target.
- **Materials**: Lambert for the cars and the ball (the flat variant's albedo and emission, no
  clearcoat, specular or environment), unlit goals, no shadow code (`shadowMap.enabled` false).
  The turf is low's unlit turf from a 64² grain (`liteGrain`, a few ms) instead of the 512² blade
  field (~140 ms here, seconds on the TV's CPU), fixed mowing stripes, no field map.
- **Stadium**: no netting, no glare round the lamp heads, no crowd mesh: the people are painted on
  the stand rows (a hash per seat: a shirt on the tread, a figure with a head on the riser),
  band-limited by the seat's footprint (`fwidth`): under ~3 px a seat each seat is its own mean
  colour, under ~1.5 px the crowd's mean, so the far stands show no moire.
- **Effects**: 128 particles, each at most ~2.3 degrees across from the camera (about a dozen
  pixels), no flash shells; additive materials in one pass (`forceSinglePass`), team tints as
  uniforms and wheel / pad instance buffers past the uniform-buffer limit so every car shares its
  programs (`SHARED_INSTANCING_CAPACITY`).
- **CPU** (`cpuLite`): `html.tg-superlow` takes the HUD's blur, shadows and decorative animations
  off (opaque panels), the boost and speed readouts update at 10 Hz, the audio has no convolver
  room, the ball prediction refreshes every 8 ticks (the bots read it by age; the wasm's 720-tick
  horizon is unchanged), and going online shows one line ("Super low: online matches may not run
  smoothly on this device"). Physics stays at 120 Hz.

### Which devices start on it (`bootTier.ts`)

Before the renderer exists: `#superlow` in the URL, or the tab's recovery after a second lost
device, force it for that page load (never saved); then a GPU of this device that fell back to
super low (`tierGuard.ts readSuperlowBoot`: the ceilings are keyed by a backend that does not

pick only for a signed-in player with Settings > Experimental's switch on: anyone else's saved pick

ceiling, a fallback, the guard) is never saved (`tierPrefsAfter`, `Game.persistPrefs`), which also
