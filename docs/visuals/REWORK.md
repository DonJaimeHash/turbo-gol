# Visual rework: a realistic stadium, code only

Goal: the look of the owner's concept images (a floodlit night stadium in a harbour, wet striped
grass, a steel cage with wire netting, floodlight towers, stands with a crowd, banners and LED
boards, a city and harbour behind; and the same stadium in other settings), with **no asset
files** (every mesh, texture and sound is generated by this repository's code) and **without making
the game slow**.

It is based on a measured prototype (a throwaway lab outside the repo, 2026-10-20). That study scored
closeness to the concept at 12/100 for the game before this work and 48/100 for the first prototype.
It estimates about 70/100 is reachable on the high tier (about 3 ms of GPU at 1600x900 on a Turing
desktop GPU; the game used 1.2 ms) and about 60/100 on the low tier (about 1.2 ms).

## Quality tiers (`src/render/quality.ts`)

Three tiers, one table of switches (`TIERS`) that every view reads:

| | low (Performance) | high (default on desktop) | ultra (opt-in) |
|---|---|---|---|
| AA | FXAA | MSAA x4 | MSAA x4 |
| bloom | none (glow from geometry) | emissive bloom | emissive bloom |
| resolution | pixel ratio 1 | pixel budget + dynamic resolution | bigger budget + dynamic resolution |
| shadows | none (ground blobs) | 2 keys, 2048 | 2 keys, 4096 |
| turf | flat filtered, damp sheen, 2 streak heads | relief, puddles, 4 streak heads | + grass blades near the camera |
| post | grade, vignette | grade, vignette | + GTAO, SSR on wet turf, lens glare |
| stadium | lighter crowd, no fine netting | full | + floodlight beams |

Phones and tablets start on low, desktops on high, the WebGL2 fallback on low unless the player
chose a tier. Saves from before stored 0 (high) or 1 (performance). Low has no shadow maps
(since 2026-10-21): the cars and the ball keep their ground blobs, and no material samples a shadow
map (see "Owner feedback" below).

## Themes (`src/render/theme.ts`)

A theme is data: the same generators and shaders read it. Shared facts live in `theme.ts`: the two key
lights, fill, floodlights, wetness, fog, tone mapping and grade. Each view keeps its own per-theme
parameters next to its code, keyed by `ThemeId`. The light count and material variants never change
between themes, so a theme switch only re-bakes, rewrites uniforms and repaints the board
canvas; it never recompiles shaders. Gameplay never reads a theme.

Themes: `harbour-night` (the default), `harbour-day`, `canyon-sunset`, `alpine-day`. The Stadium
row picks one (saved with the prefs; an unknown saved id reads as the night harbour). Each has a
world preset and a skyline (harbour, canyon or alpine: a baked far part and, except the canyon, a
live near layer that the world swaps in by geometry), a ThemeWorld, a stadium palette, a turf look
and its place's board content (`stadium/content.ts`: invented local sponsors, names and
canvas-path emblems, and the place's main and secondary sponsor for the big side-wall boards; the
two harbour themes share the harbour's, and the harbour's names and emblems appear nowhere else).

`city-dusk` and `railyard-night` were removed on 2026-10-21 (the owner found them too bright and
reflective "for now"). To bring them back, start from commit 32aabad: `world/city.ts`,
`world/railyard.ts`, `world/railyardMaterial.ts`, their KIND ranges (TOWER / DECK, BRICK /
RAILBED) and branches in `bakeMaterials.ts`, the world's per-skyline ground / proxy overrides,
their ThemeWorld, preset, palette, turf look, content set, emblems (skyline, neonStar, wheel, gear,
chimney), menu names and `tests/railyard.test.mjs`.

## Module layout

| folder | owns | built from |
|---|---|---|
| `src/render/world/` | sky (gradient, clouds, stars, moon or sun), backdrop generators (harbour, canyon...), the bake of both into a cube map + PMREM environment, cached in IndexedDB | the old sky dome + environment |
| `src/render/turf/` | pitch material (blade texture baked at load, stripes, wear, wetness, puddles, floodlight pools and streaks, relief), grass blades (ultra) | the old floor material |
| `src/render/stadium/` | cage (steel arches, hex wire netting on the shell), LED boards with a big sponsor board on each side wall (each place's own content; the banners went on 2026-10-21), stands and crowd, floodlight towers and lamp heads, beams (ultra) | the old truss, cones, stands, masts, halos, lamp bars, glass |
| `src/render/post/` | render pipeline per tier (bloom, grade, vignette, FXAA, GTAO / SSR / flare on ultra), key lights and shadows | the old `applyQuality` chain |
| `Car.ts`, `Ball.ts` | paint with clearcoat flakes, tyres, glass, the panelled ball | same files |

Goals, goal frames and boost pads stay in `Arena.ts`. The collision mesh (`src/sim/arenaMesh.ts`) does not
change: everything drawn on the walls coincides with the shell or sits outside it, and the roof stays
flat because the physics ceiling is flat.

## Contracts every module keeps

- WebGPU and the automatic WebGL2 fallback (TSL only; compute needs a fallback path).
- Glow: bloom reads only the `emissive` MRT target. Every unlit or transparent material sets
  `mrtNode = glowMrt(...)` or `noGlowMrt()` (`materials.ts`). On low there is no bloom, so glow must
  come from geometry and HDR colour there.
- NaN hygiene: saturate every uv, fade and `pow` base. Verify with `#nancheck`.
- The scene renders through the pipeline's PassNode only; tone mapping and sRGB are applied once in
  `renderOutput`.
- No allocation per frame. Static work is baked at load behind the loading overlay. Hidden meshes
  get a warm-up hook (`Game.setWarm`).
- Draw calls: at most 40 on low and 60 on high. One draw per module element is the house style
  (instance or merge).
- Phones: everything the low tier and the WebGL2 fallback use must work on a low-end mobile GPU.
  `#lowgpu` in the URL emulates one on a desktop (WebGL2 without OES_texture_float_linear,
  EXT_color_buffer_float, EXT_float_blend..., capped limits); `#diag` shows the backend, GPU,
  extensions, limits and every shader or GPU error on screen, for a screenshot from the phone.
- Gameplay readability: markings, goal frames, boost pads, ball marker and car silhouettes stay
  legible. Team colours keep their colour-blind separation.
- Original content only. No commercial game names, logos, car designs or layouts. Board
  text is invented and drawn with code.

## Phases

Status (2026-10-21): phases 1-4 are done on `feat/visual-rework`; phase 5's review and docs are
done (see "Final review" below); the owner tested a preview on phones and asked for changes (see
"Owner feedback" at the end), which are in.

1. **Foundation** (no visual change).
   - The three tiers, prefs migration and the menu.
   - Dynamic resolution.
   - Theme plumbing.
   - Move the old sky, environment, floor, stadium and pipeline code into the module folders above,
     as the seams for phase 2.
2. **Modules** (in parallel, each in its folder, ported from the prototype with the critic's fixes).
   - World: sky and backdrop bake.
   - Turf.
   - Stadium.
   - Post and lighting: two shadowed keys, grade per theme, hot lamp heads with tight glare, and the
     ultra extras.
   - Car and ball materials.
3. **Integration and tuning.** Done:
   - The integrated captures' faults: the ultra lens glare keys on white-hot emissive only (no red
     haze from the goal frame); the wet floodlight reflections are soft anisotropic streaks toward
     the camera; the whitish fog on the damp turf (the keys' GGX highlight and the probe's hot lamp
     panels on the sheen, and the broad streak lobes seen from high cameras) is gone; the dark band
     across the near pitch (a mowing stripe) is soft; round the goals the cage is netting and the
     goal net draws from both sides (no black box).
   - Toward the concept: the floodlight pools shape the key lights and sit under their shadows
     (car shadows read), a dominant night key, a warmer olive turf, a brighter blue sky, a lit and
     less saturated crowd, fainter goal-mouth glow, a softer vignette.

   - The WebGL2 fallback starts on low unless the player chose a tier; `#webgl2` forces it for

     first frame on WebGPU high is ready in ~2.7 s instead of ~5.9 s. Boot marks in main.ts.
   - The Friendly screen's Stadium row (the theme picker).
   - Measured: see "Measuring" below and ARCHITECTURE.md "Rendering budget".
4. **The other five themes.** Done (`city-dusk` and `railyard-night` were removed again on
   2026-10-21, see "Themes"): `city-dusk` (a car-park roof in a city at dusk: towers with

## Measuring

- `#stats` in the URL: GPU ms (timestamp queries) and draw calls in the F3 line.
- Compare tiers at the chase camera on the same machine.
- Report: GPU ms per tier at 1600x900 and at the screen's size, draw calls, triangles, load time
  (bake and first-frame compile).

Targets on the reference Turing GPU at 1600x900:

| tier | GPU | draw calls |
|---|---|---|
| low | ≤ 1.5 ms | ≤ 40 |
| high | ≤ 3.5 ms | ≤ 60 |
| ultra | ≤ 5 ms | — |

First load ≤ 4 s on WebGPU high with a warm cache.

Final review results (free play at kickoff, chase camera, median of three 120-frame means, the
GPU shared with other sessions; night harbour, every theme within ~0.1 ms of it):

| tier | GPU | draw calls | triangles | textures |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| low | 0.82 ms | 28 | 90k (alpine 151k) | 77 MB |
| high | 1.97 ms | 42 | 136k (alpine 196k) | 175 MB |
| ultra | 3.15 ms (alpine 3.23) | 53 | 371k (alpine 432k) | 399 MB |

First load (production build, WebGPU, to the first frame finished on the GPU): high 1.8 s with a
warm world cache, 3.0-4.0 s with an empty one; low 1.6 s warm. A cached theme switch takes
55-150 ms, a re-bake 40-470 ms, the first bake of a session ~1-1.4 s. The cache keeps six bakes
(every theme of a tier; least recently used out first).

Open against the targets:
- A 3v3 match frame draws 51 calls on low and 72 on high (each car: body, wheels, and both again
  per shadowed key); GPU time stays within budget (low 0.85 ms, high 2.0 ms, ultra 3.3 ms).
  Options: instance every car body and every wheel set into one draw each, or a single shadowed key
  for the bots' cars.
- A tier switch still compiles its pipelines synchronously (1-3 s on the GPU, in the menu).
  Shader sizes: turf 63 KB, car 60 KB, ball 52 KB, four lit stadium materials 30-34 KB each.
- The canyon's far part is built synchronously during a bake (its terrain holds the main thread
  ~0.3 s); only the alpine range is built in slices.

Still open from the theme work (content and art direction, for the owner):
- The alpine stands are the tall concrete-frame stands in timber colours (the concept has low
  gabled stands); the canyon's side walls sit mostly behind the stand canopies in the chase view.
- The red goal frame against the warm canyon backdrop, and a ball lobbed against the alpine snow,
  read with less contrast than on the harbour (still clear in the captures).

## Final review

The branch was reviewed as a whole after the alpine and railyard merges (WebGPU and the WebGL2
fallback, an empty, cached and unavailable IndexedDB, tier and theme switches during a 3v3 match,
the Stadium and Quality rows by mouse and keyboard, saves from before the tiers and themes).
Fixed: every bake kept its far skyline mesh alive through the renderer's render objects (~10 MB
of JS heap and a uniform buffer per baking theme switch); a replaced environment probe kept its
live copy's render objects; the world cache held 3 bakes for six themes and evicted by age
(now 6, least recently used); dead helpers of the old renderer; duplicated CPU noise helpers;
these docs.

## Owner feedback (2026-10-21), the render track

The owner tried a preview on phones. Changes (render side; the loading screens, the settings in
the pause menu and the ball's shadow by day are the UI track's):

- **A phone that drew no pitch** (Huawei, Kirin 710F / Mali-G51, Android; an iPhone 15 was fine):
  on low the pitch was dark grey without markings, the car a blob without a body, no ball. Those
  are the only three materials that receive shadows, so the one path they share is the shadow-map
  sampling; three r186 even takes an Android-only depth-compare fallback for it on WebGPU, and the
  WebGL2 path samples a `sampler2DShadow` in dynamic branches. The float formats were audited and
  ruled out: every texture and target the low tier and WebGL2 use is RGBA8 or half float (blade
  field, field and pool maps, light map, world cube / PMREM / proxy, board atlas, post targets),
  and the game renders the same under `#lowgpu`. Low has no shadow maps now (the cars and the
  ball keep their ground blobs), which also frees the 34 MB shadow map and the shadow pass on
  phones. Not reproducible on a desktop driver: `#diag` on the phone will tell (backend, GPU,
  extensions, limits, any shader or GPU error).
- **Boards**: a big board three panels wide (~31 m, the same height) in the middle of each side
  wall alternates between the place's main and secondary sponsor every 30 s, half a period out of
  step with the other boards; every board changes every 30 s instead of 8, a whole panel at a
  time, with a short dip instead of a slide. The panels tile the outline exactly (36). Same
  material, texture and draw. The LED face is satin (its gloss mirrored the lamp heads across the
  names).
- **Calmer night pitch**: the night harbour is damp, not soaked (wetness 0.32, was 0.55), with
  shallower puddles and fainter streaks, and every tier evaluates half the streak heads (low 2,
  high and ultra 4). On the desktop GPU the frame time is the same within noise (A/B interleaved,
  1600x900, median of 3 x 120 frames: low 0.807 / 0.814 ms, high 1.839 / 1.833, ultra 3.094 /
  3.078); the saving is for ALU-bound phones.
- **Themes**: `city-dusk` and `railyard-night` removed (see "Themes"); `harbour-day` stays (the
  owner named only the city rooftop and the rail yard).
- **No hanging banners** on any stadium (one draw less).

Measured after the changes (same method as the final review, night harbour): low 0.82 ms / 24
draws / 37 MB of textures, high 1.83 ms / 41 / 160 MB, ultra 3.04 ms / 52 / 374 MB.
